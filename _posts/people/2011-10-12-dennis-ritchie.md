## Summary

Dennis MacAlistair Ritchie (September 9, 1941 – c. October 12, 2011) was an American computer scientist who created the C programming language and, with his Bell Labs colleague Ken Thompson, co-created the Unix operating system. He was found dead at his home in Berkeley Heights, New Jersey, on or about October 12, 2011, at the age of 70, after several years of frail health following treatment for prostate cancer and heart disease. His former colleague Rob Pike announced the death.

Ritchie's passing came just eight days after that of Apple co-founder [Steve Jobs]({{ '/news/people/steve-jobs/' | relative_url }}), and commentators noted the contrast between the two: Jobs was a globally recognized public figure, while Ritchie's work was largely invisible to the public yet underpinned much of modern computing—including the very devices for which Jobs was celebrated. The programmer Rob Pike and others observed that C and Unix form the foundation on which operating systems from macOS and iOS to Linux and Android are built.

## Career and Contributions

Ritchie was born in Bronxville, New York, and educated at Harvard University, where he studied physics and applied mathematics. He joined the Bell Telephone Laboratories Computing Sciences Research Center in 1967, where he spent his entire career, later working under Lucent Technologies before retiring in 2007.

**The C programming language**: Between 1969 and 1973, Ritchie developed C, building on Thompson's earlier B language. C combined low-level access to memory and hardware with high-level structure and portability, and it became one of the most widely used programming languages in history. Its influence is direct and pervasive: C++, C#, Objective-C, Java, and many other languages inherit its syntax and design.

**Unix**: Working with Thompson from 1969 onward, Ritchie helped design and build Unix. A pivotal step came when Thompson rewrote the Unix kernel in C, making the operating system portable across different hardware platforms—a break from the prior norm of systems tied to a single machine. This portability allowed Unix and its descendants to spread across the computing world.

**"The C Programming Language"**: In 1978, Ritchie and Brian Kernighan published *The C Programming Language*, universally known as "K&R." The book became the standard reference for the language and a model of technical writing; its opening "hello, world" example entered programming culture.

## Honors

- **Turing Award (1983)**: Ritchie shared the Association for Computing Machinery's Turing Award—often called the "Nobel Prize of computing"—with Ken Thompson for their development of generic operating systems theory and specifically the Unix operating system.
- **National Medal of Technology (1998)**: Ritchie and Thompson received the award from President Bill Clinton for co-inventing Unix and C.
- **Japan Prize (2011)**: In the year of his death, Ritchie and Thompson were awarded the Japan Prize for Information and Communications for their work on Unix.

## Significance

Dennis Ritchie's C and Unix are among the most consequential creations in the history of computing. Decades after their introduction, C remained one of the most-used programming languages, and Unix-derived systems ran the majority of the world's servers, supercomputers, and smartphones. His death in October 2011, alongside that of Steve Jobs, prompted widespread reflection on the difference between visible technological celebrity and the largely anonymous foundational work that makes it possible. As many tributes noted, few people had heard of Ritchie, yet nearly everyone used the technologies he made possible every day.
