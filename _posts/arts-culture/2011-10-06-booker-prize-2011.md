**Date**: October 6, 2011 | **Category**: Arts & Culture

## Summary

Julian Barnes won the 2011 Man Booker Prize for *The Sense of an Ending*, a contemporary literary novel published in August 2011. The work follows Tony Webster, a retired man who confronts his past when a lawyer's letter prompts him to reconnect with a former girlfriend and the memory of a school friend's suicide decades earlier. The novel's exploration of aging, memory, and self-delusion — told through a subtly unreliable narrator — marked Barnes's first Booker Prize win after five decades as a major literary figure, despite his earlier dismissal of the prize as "posh bingo."

## Significance

**Breakthrough Recognition**: *The Sense of an Ending* represented a significant milestone for Barnes, one of England's most celebrated novelists, who had long dismissed the Booker Prize's selection process but won the 2011 award with head judge Stella Rimington (former MI5 Director General) praising the work as "exquisitely written, subtly plotted and reveals new depths with each reading."

**Literary Innovation**: The novel's narrative technique — an elderly man's selective, unreliable recollection of his youth — resonated with readers and critics as a meditation on how memory constructs (and distorts) identity. The unresolved tension between Tony's perception and objective truth became a hallmark of the work's interpretive depth.

**Later Impact**: The novel was adapted into a feature film released in 2017, directed by Ritesh Batra, extending its cultural reach beyond the literary community.
