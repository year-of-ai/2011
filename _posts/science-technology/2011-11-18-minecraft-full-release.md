## Summary

On November 18, 2011, the Swedish studio Mojang released *Minecraft* Java Edition 1.0.0, the first officially complete version of the sandbox game, during MineCon 2011 at the Mandalay Bay resort in Las Vegas. The release ended roughly two and a half years of public development that had begun with an early alpha in 2009 and a paid beta in December 2010. Creator Markus "Notch" Persson symbolically pulled a lever on stage to launch the finished game before an audience of about 4,500 attendees at the first official *Minecraft* convention.

## From Alpha to 1.0

*Minecraft* had been sold to the public throughout its development, an unusual "early access" model in which players paid to play unfinished builds. By the time of the full release, the game had already sold more than four million copies and registered many millions of accounts, an extraordinary commercial success for an independently produced title. Version 1.0 was the last release Persson personally led before he handed lead development of the game to Jens "Jeb" Bergensten, allowing Persson to step back from day-to-day design.

## What 1.0 Added

The 1.0 release turned *Minecraft* from an open-ended construction toy into a game with defined goals. It introduced "The End," a final dimension inhabited by the Ender Dragon boss, whose defeat triggered the game's first proper ending and a scrolling credits sequence. The update also finalized brewing and potions, an enchanting system for tools and weapons, animal breeding, and a permadeath "Hardcore" mode, building on the survival and crafting mechanics added during the beta.

## Significance

The full release cemented *Minecraft*'s transformation from a niche indie project into a global cultural phenomenon. Its procedurally generated, block-based worlds and emphasis on open-ended creativity influenced a generation of games and became a fixture of online video and education. *Minecraft* would go on to become the best-selling video game of all time, and Mojang's rising value led Microsoft to acquire the studio for $2.5 billion in 2014.
