**Date**: October 19–28, 2011 | **Category**: Society & Economics

## Summary

The St. Louis Cardinals defeated the Texas Rangers 4–3 in a thrilling seven-game World Series in October 2011, clinching their 11th World Series championship. The series is remembered for one of baseball's greatest comebacks: trailing three games to two and facing elimination in Game 6 at Arlington, the Cardinals scored twice in the ninth and tenth innings before David Freese's walk-off home run in the 11th sent the series to a decisive Game 7, which the Cardinals won 6–2 at Busch Stadium. Freese earned World Series MVP honors with a postseason record 21 RBIs, while Albert Pujols contributed three home runs in Game 3—a feat shared only by Babe Ruth and Reggie Jackson.

## Significance

**Wild Card Redemption**: The Cardinals became the first wild-card team to win the World Series in seven years after overcoming a 10.5-game deficit in the standings on August 24. Their 23–9 finish drove an improbable playoff run that culminated in championship glory, exemplifying resilience and clutch performance under pressure.

**Game 6 Historic Drama**: In one of baseball's most iconic moments, the Rangers were "one strike away" from winning their first championship twice in Game 6 — yet the Cardinals refused to yield. Freese's 11th-inning solo home run off Mark Lowe at 11:39 pm forced Game 7 and preserved the series; few World Series games rival this combination of desperation, recovery, and redemption.

**Manager's Milestone**: Tony La Russa won his third World Series championship and retired immediately after the victory, joining only eight others in history with three or more titles — cementing his Hall of Fame legacy.

**Rangers' Consecutive Heartbreak**: Texas became the first franchise to lose back-to-back World Series appearances (2010, 2011) since the Atlanta Braves (1991–1992), postponing the franchise's first championship.

**Postseason Excellence**: The Cardinals' statistical dominance included Lance Berkman's NL Comeback Player of the Year award, Pujols's rare three-home-run game, Nelson Cruz's eight homers across the postseason (tied for record), and Chris Carpenter's franchise record of four postseason wins—a well-balanced championship team.
