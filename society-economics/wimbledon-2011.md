# 2011 Wimbledon Championships

**Category:** Society & Economics

**Key figures:** Novak Djokovic (men's champion), Petra Kvitová (women's champion), Rafael Nadal (men's finalist), Maria Sharapova (women's finalist), Andy Murray (men's semifinalist)

## Summary

The 2011 Wimbledon Championships, held June 20 – July 3, 2011, at the All England Lawn Tennis and Croquet Club in Wimbledon, London, marked a historic moment in tennis with two first-time singles champions crowned on the grass courts. Novak Djokovic defeated Rafael Nadal 6–4, 6–1, 1–6, 6–3 to claim his maiden Wimbledon title and the ATP World No. 1 ranking, while Czech teenager Petra Kvitová upset Maria Sharapova 6–3, 6–4 to win her first Grand Slam title at age 21. The simultaneous emergence of two maiden Wimbledon champions — one from Serbia, one from the Czech Republic — signaled a broadening of competition at the top of both the men's and women's tours.

## Tournament Overview

### Conditions and Draw

The 2011 Championships was the 125th edition of Wimbledon. Play began June 20 and concluded with the women's final on July 2 and the men's final on July 3. Weather conditions were typical for the British summer — overcast with several rain delays — but the main show courts benefited from the Centre Court retractable roof first installed in 2009. Total prize money for the event was £14.6 million, an increase of 5.2% over the previous year.

The men's draw featured the sport's dominant triumvirate of the era: Roger Federer (seeded 3), Rafael Nadal (seeded 1), and Novak Djokovic (seeded 2). Djokovic arrived having already won the Australian Open in January 2011 and going 41–0 for the year before losing to Federer in the French Open semifinals. His grass-court record, however, was less distinguished than his clay-court form.

### Men's Singles

Djokovic's march through the draw was formidable. He dropped only one set before the final, coming through a spirited quarterfinal against the 18-year-old Australian qualifier Bernard Tomic, 6–2, 3–6, 6–3, 7–5. Roger Federer, seeking a seventh Wimbledon title that would have equalled Pete Sampras's Open-era record, fell to Jo-Wilfried Tsonga in the quarterfinals in a stunning upset — Tsonga becoming the first player ever to defeat Federer at a Grand Slam after losing the opening two sets.

The men's final on July 3 pitted Djokovic against Nadal, the reigning champion having won Wimbledon in 2008 and 2010. The match lasted 2 hours and 36 minutes. Djokovic controlled the first two sets with aggressive baseline play, lost the third set as Nadal fought back, then reasserted his dominance in the fourth to claim the 6–4, 6–1, 1–6, 6–3 victory. It was Djokovic's third Grand Slam title (after the 2008 and 2011 Australian Opens) and his first at Wimbledon. By winning, he displaced Nadal as ATP World No. 1 — a ranking he would hold almost continuously through 2012 and beyond.

Djokovic's 2011 season is widely considered one of the greatest single-season performances in tennis history: he finished the year 70–6, won three Grand Slams, and spent 53 weeks at No. 1.

### Women's Singles

The women's draw produced an even larger upset. Petra Kvitová, a 21-year-old from Fulnek in the Czech Republic, had reached the Wimbledon semifinals in 2010 but was seeded eighth and not considered a favorite. She had a lefty serve-and-volley game ideally suited to grass, but her consistency on other surfaces had been questioned.

Kvitová defeated a sequence of higher-ranked opponents with increasingly impressive grass-court tennis. In the final she faced Maria Sharapova — seeded fifth and a former Wimbledon champion (2004) — and dominated from the first game. The match lasted just 59 minutes: Kvitová won 6–3, 6–4, striking 27 winners and committing only 5 unforced errors. The lopsided margin belied the quality of both players; Kvitová simply played at a level that left Sharapova no answer.

Kvitová's victory made her:
- The first Czech woman to win the Wimbledon singles title since Jana Novotná in 1998
- The first left-handed women's singles champion at Wimbledon since Martina Navratilova in 1990
- The first Grand Slam singles champion of either gender born in the 1990s (born March 8, 1990)

### Doubles and Other Events

Czech players featured prominently across the event. Květa Peschke, partnering Slovenia's Katarina Srebotnik, won the women's doubles — her first Grand Slam title — while Iveta Benešová, partnering Austria's Jürgen Melzer, won the mixed doubles. In men's doubles, the American twins Bob and Mike Bryan claimed their second Wimbledon title, defeating Robert Lindstedt and Horia Tecău in the final.

## Cultural and Historical Context

The 2011 Championships coincided with considerable public interest in Wimbledon due to Andy Murray's anticipated run. Murray, the British No. 1, was playing at a time of intense national hope for a British men's champion — a drought stretching back to Fred Perry in 1936. Murray reached the semifinals before falling to Nadal in four sets, raising expectations that were fulfilled the following year when he won the Olympic gold medal at Wimbledon in 2012 and the Wimbledon title itself in 2013.

The event was also notable for the increasing prominence of the Centre Court roof, which allowed matches that might have been rained out or suspended to continue into the evening. Its installation in 2009 had been a significant investment of approximately £80 million and transformed Wimbledon's relationship with British summer weather.

## Significance

The 2011 Wimbledon Championships signaled a realignment in global tennis that would define the sport's landscape through the mid-2010s. Djokovic's victory was the first tangible sign of his capacity to dislodge Federer and Nadal from their decade-long dominance, inaugurating what analysts called the "Big Three" era in which all three players competed at world No. 1 simultaneously. Kvitová's victory demonstrated that the WTA women's tour was entering a period of genuine competitive depth, with players from Eastern Europe — particularly the Czech Republic — rising to challenge the established order of Venus and Serena Williams, Sharapova, and Kim Clijsters.

Both champions demonstrated sustained excellence in the years following: Djokovic won a second Wimbledon in 2014, 2015, 2018, 2019, and later titles through the 2020s, eventually surpassing Federer's record of Grand Slam titles. Kvitová won a second Wimbledon title in 2014.

The 2011 Championships took place within a year of extraordinary sporting competition globally, including the [Cricket World Cup 2011](cricket-world-cup-2011.md), won by India for the first time in 28 years, and the [Rugby World Cup 2011](rugby-world-cup-2011.md), where New Zealand ended a 24-year title drought on home soil.
