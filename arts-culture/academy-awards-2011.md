# 83rd Academy Awards

**Category**: Arts & Culture  
**Key figures**: The King's Speech, Tom Hooper, Colin Firth, Natalie Portman, Christian Bale, Melissa Leo, James Franco, Anne Hathaway

## Summary

The 83rd Academy Awards ceremony, presented by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences, was held on February 27, 2011, at the Kodak Theatre in Hollywood, Los Angeles, honoring the best films of 2010. The British historical drama *The King's Speech*, directed by Tom Hooper, won Best Picture along with three other awards, leading the night alongside Christopher Nolan's *Inception*, which also won four. The ceremony was co-hosted by actors James Franco and Anne Hathaway, a pairing intended to draw younger viewers that drew mixed reviews.

## The King's Speech

*The King's Speech*—the story of King George VI's efforts to overcome a stammer with the help of speech therapist Lionel Logue ahead of the Second World War—entered the ceremony with a leading 12 nominations and converted four of them:

- **Best Picture**
- **Best Director** — Tom Hooper
- **Best Actor** — Colin Firth, as King George VI
- **Best Original Screenplay** — David Seidler

Firth's win capped a season of near-universal acclaim, and the screenwriter David Seidler, then 73, became one of the oldest recipients of the writing award, having drawn on his own childhood stammer.

## Other Major Winners

- **Best Actress** — Natalie Portman, for *Black Swan*
- **Best Supporting Actor** — Christian Bale, for *The Fighter*
- **Best Supporting Actress** — Melissa Leo, for *The Fighter*
- **Best Animated Feature** — *Toy Story 3*
- **Inception** — won four technical awards (Cinematography, Sound Editing, Sound Mixing, and Visual Effects), tying *The King's Speech* for the most wins of the night

## Reception

The telecast drew approximately 37.9 million viewers in the United States, a decline of about 9 percent from the previous year. The decision to pair Franco and Hathaway as hosts, aimed at attracting a younger audience, met with mixed critical response; several reviewers found the broadcast flat and singled out Franco's low-energy performance, while others credited Hathaway's enthusiasm.

## Significance

The 83rd Academy Awards affirmed a strong year for the prestige historical drama and for British cinema, with *The King's Speech* triumphing over the technologically ambitious *Inception* and the digital-era drama *The Social Network*, which had been the critics' front-runner for much of the season. The result was later cited in debates about the Academy's preference for traditional, uplifting subject matter over more formally daring work. The ceremony also marked a notable, and much-discussed, experiment in hosting that the Academy did not repeat, returning to more established emcees in subsequent years.
